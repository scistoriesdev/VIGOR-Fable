Personal Introduction

I am a UMass Chan Medical Student taking a research year as a Clinical Research Assistant in the Department of Dermatology. As a member of Dr. John Harris’ Clinical Research team, I support the research efforts of departmental faculty investigating dermatologic conditions including vitiligo and hidradenitis suppurativa. My role on the team involves screening and consenting study participants, coordinating research study visits, obtaining samples, documenting research activities, and maintaining regulatory documents among others.

Your Background and Journey

I graduated with a bachelor’s degree in biology and a minor in economics. Coming from a background where my mother’s career is in higher education as a professor and my grandmother was a teacher, I have always valued education and did work tutoring and teaching before medical school. These same values drive my commitment to patient education as a vehicle for spreading awareness about disease processes. I believe in increasing healthcare access by partnering with patients to empower them to advocate for better outcomes for themselves and their families. As a medical student with an interest in dermatology, I am passionate about dermatologic conditions that have a higher impact and prevalence among women and people of color. In the future, I see myself caring for such patients and addressing the skin needs of those who struggle to gain access to comprehensive healthcare either due to barriers caused by where they live or their financial situation. Being a member of the VIGOR research team equips me to better understand the scientific basis of disease and this will enable me to provide more holistic care and better explanations to my future patients’ questions. Knowing I am playing a role in improving the quality of life for countless individuals through this study gives me a sense of fulfilment. This is because the study is helping to elucidate the root causes of vitiligo and understanding that these data will help form the foundation of future preventative and therapeutic measures is extremely exciting and rewarding.

Outside of Work

Outside of work, I am a wife and mother to a one-year-old daughter. Though parenting in medical school can be challenging, spending time with my family is a joy and a grounding force for me. When I am not at school, work, or at home, I dedicate my time to community service, patient education and advocacy. In the course of my medical education, I have served as a member of the Student National Medical Association (SNMA) E-board as a community service liaison. To this end, I have hosted, organized, and participated in many community health events in partnerships with other organizations, centering on medical screenings and patient education. My hobbies include creative writing, traveling, sewing, singing, gardening, cooking, and trying new cuisines. Below is a picture of me swimming with a dolphin in Ocho Rios, Jamacia.
