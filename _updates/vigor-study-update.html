It's an exciting time to be a part of the VIGOR study! We are closing in on our goal of signing up 300 families and have started to work with each of you to make sure all family members that are willing to participate are included. While we proceed toward our 300-family goal, we wanted to update you on what to expect in the next weeks and to get your feedback about a few additional opportunities for the study that we are considering.

Remarkable Enrollment Milestone

Your interest is contagious and reflected in 240 families that have already signed up. These families include 685 total participants that span from 41 of the 50 states, with Massachusetts, California, and Florida leading the way. We also have families in 6 of Canada’s 10 provinces. Women have been more eager to sign up than men at a 2:1 ratio, and with our goal of equal representation, we are looking for your help to balance the group by recruiting more men.

In the coming weeks we will work with you to sign up all of your family members and ensure that we correctly identify the relationships between participants (siblings, parents, children, cousins, aunt/uncles, etc). Once we have this information, we will provide you with a pedigree chart, or “family tree” of sorts, illustrating these relationships as well as who has vitiligo and who doesn’t. After reviewing your family relationships, we may ask if there are others who could be recruited, based on potential opportunities we see in the pedigree.

Addressing Participant Concerns

Some participants have included questions in the comment box of our survey that we would like to answer for everyone:

Two asked about privacy and who will be able to access the data:

  1. “How and with whom will my personal information be shared?
  2. “My main concern is about privacy.”

We are using high quality, validated clinical trial software that supports thousands of clinical trials to store your private “identifiable” information. We plan to submit your non-identifiable genomic data to secure servers at the National Institutes of Health (NIH, our country’s biomedical research division), which is only accessible by investigators that have been trained in human genetics research, so none of your confidential information will be shared. We only use your confidential information to communicate with you, which is only available to the clinical team and will only be used for shipping at-home sampling kits and to reach out to communicate with you when necessary.

Nine of you expressed some discomfort with drawing blood:

  1. “I get very nervous to take my own blood or anything like that, so collecting samples of that fashion makes me very nervous.”
  2. “Ok with everything but blood drawing.”
  3. “My children are young. They will not participate in the blood draw.”
  4. “Don't want to give blood”
  5. “…I react to anything invasive, including blood draws…”
  6. “…I am willing to participate in your study, but I am not comfortable collecting blood samples at home. I would be willing to collect other samples at home and willing to go to a lab to have blood drawn if necessary.”
  7. “No blood collection, please.”
  8. “Do i have to do the blood drawing since i am extremely uncomfortable doing so?”
  9. “I am comfortable with the skin tape, micro patches and the saliva collection, but not with the blood device.”

We understand your concerns. Because the small amounts of blood that we will collect is so useful for our analysis, the co-director of the study Manuel Garber made videos in English and Spanish using the Tasso device to show how it works. Please check them and let us know if you have any concerns or questions at vigor@umassmed.edu. Manuel and others in the team are happy to provide a live demonstration or a question-and-answer session if it helps.

Your Input Matters

As we progress, we are keen on incorporating your thoughts into the study's development. We seek your input on two significant considerations:

Utilizing Technology for Communication

We are exploring the possibility of using a dedicated application on your phone or computer for study communications, including consent forms, surveys, and at-home sampling updates. We would like to know if this is something you would welcome or prefer not to have.

Stress Measurement Through Hair Samples

Stress has been reported as a potential trigger for vitiligo and other autoimmune conditions. While surveys will be sent every three months to capture changes in stress levels, we have identified a test that can measure stress levels easily through hair samples. We believe this could be a more convenient and non-invasive approach.

Your opinions and preferences are integral to shaping the future of the study. We invite you to share your thoughts through this by completing the following survey.
