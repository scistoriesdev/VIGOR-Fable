Personal Introduction

I am a graduate of UMass Chan, having earned my PhD in Immunology and Virology here in 2003. As a student, I developed a deep interest in autoimmune diseases and my dissertation research focused treatments for Type 1 Diabetes, which results from the immune system attacking and destroying the insulin-producing cells in the pancreas. The immune system is such an intricate, fine-tuned machine and its ability to keep us healthy despite all the “bad” things in the world around us is fascinating to me. Understanding what happens when the immune system “misfires” as it does in autoimmune diseases is a really challenging problem to solve, precisely because the function of the immune system is so complex. However, it is taking that challenge head-on that drives me.

Your Background and Journey

I have had an unusual career path that has included research, undergraduate teaching, and work in higher education publishing. While each of these career roles have been very different from each other, they do share a common thread that defines my career, namely that they have all been mission-driven, with a goal of helping people. My background has prepared me to help with the VIGOR study because this study involves aspects of all my previous positions. My formal training in immunology is important for studying vitiligo, which is an autoimmune disease itself. Additionally, there are important communication, education, and project management aspects to the VIGOR study that will require my experience in teaching and publishing. I am excited about the opportunity to utilize my background as a part of an amazing team to work on another mission-driven project. That is, to uncover the causes of vitiligo and thus help predict, prevent, and treat this condition.

Outside of Work

Outside of work, I enjoy spending time with my family. My wife is a practicing dermatologist, so I can talk about skin and immunology any time I want! However, most of our time is focused on our three kids’ and their activities. Living in southern New Hampshire, we also love to take advantage of time in the great outdoors, whether hiking, camping, or skiing. Speaking of skiing, I am an avid downhill and Nordic skier. I recently completed the American Birkebeiner Ski Marathon for the first time. It is a 50 kilometer race through rural Wisconsin and is the largest Nordic skiing race in the United States. The thousands of participants make their way through the woods of Wisconsin and finish by skiing up the snow covered main street of Hayward to the finish line (see the picture of me below approaching the finish line). It was an unforgettable experience and probably best described as the Boston Marathon of cross-country skiing. Hopefully, I will get to do the “Birkie” again in the coming years!
