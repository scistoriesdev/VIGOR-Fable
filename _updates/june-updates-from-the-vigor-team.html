🧬 What We’re Learning From Your Answers

Thanks to all of you for completing the quesionnaires that we’ve been sending you. From the 170 of you who have vitiligo and have answered the questions about your condition, we’ve been able to confirm an important finding about vitiligo: there appear to be two main types of disease onset. One starts early in life — typically around 10 years old, and another starts later — around 35 years old.

This pattern had been observed in large international studies with thousands of participants, but from your responses alone — just 170 people! — confirmed the same result. That’s an incredible testament to the quality of the data you're providing. Thank you!

The graph below shows the age at which vitiligo first appeared among the VIGOR participants. While it’s a bit technical, the key takeaway is simple: there are two distinct peaks of vitiligo onset: one in childhood, and one in adulthood.

Looking ahead, we’re now working to understand why this pattern occurs. Are there different genetic or environmental factors behind early- and late-onset vitiligo? We’re excited to dig into that next — and your continued participation will be key.

🕒 Fitbit Study Update — Help Us Track Triggers Like Stress & Illness

We’re also getting ready to send Fitbit Sense watches to all participants! While wearing the watch is completely optional, we would deeply appreciate your help if you're willing to use it.

Here’s why it matters:

  1. It helps us detect episodes of illness automatically — changes in skin temperature and resting heart rate can give us early signals of illness.
  2. It helps us understand stress — physical stress, like poor sleep or changes in activity, may contribute to the development or worsening of vitiligo.

Both illness and stress have been anecdotally linked to vitiligo onset or worsening, but they haven’t been studied systematically — until now. With your help, we hope to understand how these factors affect skin balance (homeostasis)and possibly how they trigger vitiligo in some people.

What we’ll ask you to share:

  • Resting heart rate
  • Skin temperature
  • Sleep patterns
  • Activity/step count

What we won’t collect:

  • GPS or location data
  • Any Fitbit data that you don’t want to share

We believe this is one of the most promising ways to understand how real-life experiences affect vitiligo — and we’re so grateful for your partnership.

📈 Why We’re So Excited About Fitbit Devices

To give you a real-world example of what’s possible with Fitbit health tracking, take a look at the two graphs below. One is from Manuel (our PI) and the other from a student in the lab. Both have been wearing a Fitbit since February as a trial run to help us prepare to use them in the study with all of you.

In February, Manuel came down with what was likely the flu — running a fever between 100–101.5°F for about three days. As you’ll see in the plots, the only clear spike in skin temperature across both wearers happened exactly during that time. It’s a perfect example of how these sensors can pinpoint when you might be sick — even before you realize it.

This is a perfect example to show why we’re so excited to use the Fitbit as part of this study. If we can detect illness or elevated stress as it happens, we can start to link those events to what’s happening in your skin (via tape strips) and blood samples — and finally answer questions about how external factors might trigger or worsen vitiligo.
