Personal Introduction

I am a dermatologist and physician-scientist at UMass Chan Medical School in Worcester, Massachusetts. My entire career has been dedicated to helping people with vitiligo: through advocacy to raise awareness and acceptance of vitiligo, as a physician diagnosing, counseling, and treating patients, and finally as a scientist discovering what causes vitiligo and developing better treatments. My work is really rewarding because it gives me the chance to meet so many wonderful people with vitiligo, as well as others like me who want to make the lives of people with vitiligo better. I helped bring the first FDA-approved treatment to vitiligo patients and have also developed a new treatment that is currently in testing. Manuel (Garber, the Co-Director of VIGOR) and I came up with the idea for this study, which goes beyond anything that has been done before to discover what causes vitiligo in the first place so that we might be able to prevent it.

Your Background and Journey

When I was in medical school, I did research in a Type 1 Diabetes lab, where we worked to better understand what causes the autoimmune destruction of insulin-making cells in people with this disease. I became fascinated with this question/problem of how the body attacks itself through autoimmunity and I realized that I needed to study humans to successfully achieve my goal of preventing and treating this type of disease. I became frustrated that it was very difficult to do research on Type 1 Diabetes in humans because the insulin-making cells are in the pancreas, and it is very difficult to collect samples from this organ. While in medical school, I met a patient in the hospital who had both vitiligo and Type 1 Diabetes (as well as 2 other autoimmune diseases) and realized that I could make a bigger impact on vitiligo because it is much easier to study skin in humans than it is to study the pancreas. That’s when I decided to become a dermatologist and study vitiligo. Since that moment over 15 years ago, I have been focused on vitiligo and have never looked back. The VIGOR study is a tremendous opportunity to work with both the many people who have vitiligo, and their family members to make important new discoveries about what causes vitiligo in the first place. Manuel and I were really excited to receive an important grant from the National Institutes of Health (NIH, funded by your tax dollars) to make this study possible.

I actually think these games prepared me well for what I do as a scientist and department chair – developing strategies to maximize productivity with limited resources - it’s like life!
